# R2 — The GLP-1 dose week as lived: what people wonder, when, and what a record could answer

Researched 2026-09-04 via web search (Reddit-derived aggregations, Substack diaries + comment threads, a
published thematic analysis of r/WegovyWeightLoss, 2025-26 app-comparison articles, App Store review
mining, clinical timing references). Direct reddit.com fetches are blocked from this environment, so
community voice arrives through three channels, each flagged for strength: (a) a peer-reviewed
qualitative analysis of 660 subreddit posts, (b) firsthand diaries and comment threads on open
platforms, (c) secondhand aggregations that quote forum posts (possibly paraphrased — marked as such).

---

## 1. The dose-week rhythm as lived

The physiology gives the week a shape: tirzepatide and semaglutide peak in blood roughly **24-48 hours
post-injection** and decay toward the trough just before the next shot
([Ro](https://ro.co/weight-loss/does-tirzepatide-make-you-tired/),
[Fay Nutrition](https://www.faynutrition.com/post/does-tirzepatide-make-you-feel-tired-what-to-expect)).
The community maps its days onto that curve, and the map is remarkably consistent in SHAPE while
varying per person in WHICH day each phase lands:

- **Day 0 (shot day):** often unremarkable in the hours after; some report appetite dropping fast —
  "within 5 hours of my 1st dose the food noise was gone" (first-dose account, On The Pen comment
  thread, [onthepen.substack.com](https://onthepen.substack.com/p/they-scanned-zepbound-users-brains/comments)).
- **Days 1-2 (the peak):** strongest suppression AND strongest side effects. "The first two days after
  your injection are frequently the most challenging… appetite suppression at its highest"
  ([Fay Nutrition](https://www.faynutrition.com/post/does-tirzepatide-make-you-feel-tired-what-to-expect)).
  Community fatigue reports cluster here: "spacey and unable to think clearly in the first 2 days,"
  "for the first 3 days after an injection I am so tired it impacts what I can get done"
  ([Drugs.com user reports](https://www.drugs.com/comments/tirzepatide/sfx-tiredness.html),
  [Wellorithm](https://blog.wellorithm.com/tirzepatide-tiredness-causes-coping/)). A five-month
  Zepbound diarist: "the side effects are unpleasant, especially the day after I inject a dose, but
  tolerable… a lot of indigestion, nausea, and (oddly) burping, and not exactly constipation but
  things have 'slowed down'" ([Krause, Zepbound Starting Month 5](https://stevendkrause.substack.com/p/zepbound-starting-month-5)).
- **Days 3-4 (the plateau):** the quiet middle. Constipation onset is reported at **3-7 days after
  injection** and outlasts the other GI effects
  ([Ubie doctor's timeline](https://ubiehealth.com/doctors-note/zepbound-side-effects-onset-start-timing-guides-5711e2)).
- **Days 5-7 (the waning):** hunger and food noise creep back. "As soon as I'm on my sixth day and on
  the 7th day to my next dose my candy thoughts are already coming back… scary knowing I'm approaching
  my goal weight and that noise is still there in background ready to come back when Zepbound wears
  off" (On The Pen comment thread, [onthepen.substack.com](https://onthepen.substack.com/p/they-scanned-zepbound-users-brains/comments)).
  An aggregation of Mounjaro forum accounts: "food noise resurfaces toward the end of the weekly dose
  cycle, particularly on day six or seven" and "the food noise creeps back the day before my shot"
  ([medino, Real Stories About Mounjaro and Food Noise](https://www.medino.com/article/real-stories-about-mounjaro-and-food-noise)
  — secondhand aggregation of forum posts; treat individual quotes as illustrative, not verbatim-verified).

**Consistency across people:** the phase STRUCTURE (peak → plateau → waning) is near-universal; the
day boundaries are not. Some feel nothing until a dose increase; some get no end-of-week hunger at
all; the on-a-dose-long-enough cohort reports the week flattening out ("once you tolerate a stable
dose for 2-3 months, side effects become rare and mild" —
[Ubie](https://ubiehealth.com/doctors-note/zepbound-side-effects-onset-start-timing-guides-5711e2)).
**People do learn their own pattern** — and the learning is the coping mechanism: choosing evening
injection "so side effects are felt overnight" ([SingleCare](https://www.singlecare.com/blog/best-time-to-inject-ozempic/)),
front-loading protein on peak-suppression days, planning social meals on days 5-6, treating the
day-before-shot hunger as expected rather than as failure. The published thematic analysis of 660
r/WegovyWeightLoss posts confirms side-effect management (13.3% of posts) and efficacy monitoring
(29.4%) as the dominant ongoing conversations, with the food-noise relief its own recurring motif:
"I've shut off the noise in my brain and not constantly focused on my next meal," and the GI dark
side: "the nausea and explosive diarrhea starts and lasts a solid two days"
([Plenn et al., Obesity Science & Practice 2025;11(4):e70085, PMC12327412](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12327412/)).

## 2. The dose-day morning

What people actually do/wonder on shot day, from community + clinical-reference mining:

- **"Which site this week?"** The rotation question recurs weekly and forever. The clinical answer is
  settled — abdomen/thigh/upper arm are equivalent, rotate WITHIN an area to avoid irritation and scar
  tissue ([GoodRx](https://www.goodrx.com/wegovy/how-to-take-wegovy-tips),
  [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/drugs/where-and-how-to-inject-wegovy)) — but people
  still ask "where did I inject last?" every single week, plus the folk question "does thigh hurt
  more / cause more nausea than stomach?" (thigh is widely reported as the more painful site;
  no evidence any site changes efficacy or nausea —
  [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/where-do-you-inject-semaglutide)).
- **"Does the time of day matter?"** No clinical time-of-day requirement; same day weekly is the rule;
  evening dosing is a popular self-hack so nausea rides through sleep
  ([Drugs.com](https://www.drugs.com/medical-answers/when-best-time-day-take-ozempic-3577204/),
  [SingleCare](https://www.singlecare.com/blog/best-time-to-inject-ozempic/)).
- **"With food or without?"** For injectables: doesn't matter (no label requirement). For **oral
  semaglutide (Rybelsus) this is the WHOLE daily experience**: empty stomach, ≤4 oz water, wait 30
  minutes before any food/drink/other pills, every morning
  ([rybelsus.com](https://www.rybelsus.com/taking-rybelsus/how-to-start-rybelsus.html),
  [GoodRx](https://www.goodrx.com/rybelsus/can-you-take-rybelsus-at-night)) — the oral cohort's
  recurring frictions are the 30-minute wait and sequencing against other morning meds
  (e.g. levothyroxine threads, [HealthUnlocked](https://healthunlocked.com/thyroiduk/posts/149584640/rybelsus-and-levothyroxine-when-to-take)).
- **Shot-day rituals:** many pair shot day with the weekly weigh-in and a progress photo; "Shot Day"
  is a recurring community post genre (e.g. "Mounjaro Shot Day… maintenance for 4 months" post
  titles). The weekly cadence makes shot day the natural bookkeeping day.
- **"I'm a day late — now what?"** The single most repeated logistics question. The windows are
  label-defined: tirzepatide within **96 hours**, semaglutide/Wegovy within **120 hours** (Ozempic
  commonly quoted as 5 days); past the window, skip and resume
  ([UCLA Health](https://www.uclahealth.org/news/article/missed-dose-or-more-your-glp-1-medication-heres-what-do),
  [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/drugs/missed-glp-1-dose),
  [Shotsy's own guide](https://shotsyapp.com/what-to-do-if-you-miss-a-glp-1-dose/)). People also ask
  "can I MOVE my shot day permanently?" (yes, with a minimum gap — a med-specific rule an app must not
  restate as advice, only route).

## 3. After a titration up (the 2-4 week watch)

The dose change opens a distinct anxiety window, well-documented in both directions:

- **Side effects reset:** "side effects most often start within the first week of the first injection
  and within a few days of each dose increase"
  ([Ubie](https://ubiehealth.com/doctors-note/zepbound-side-effects-onset-start-timing-guides-5711e2));
  fatigue prevalence rises with dose ([Healthline Zepbound side effects](https://www.healthline.com/health/drugs/zepbound-side-effects)).
  People watch: is this nausea the same as last titration's? How long did it last then?
- **Efficacy watch:** "is 7.5 working?" — the community expectation is that a new dose announces
  itself through quieter food noise in week 1-2; when it doesn't, the "did I get a bad pen /
  is my dose too low" spiral starts. Clinical sources push back on the timescale: "after 2 weeks you
  probably won't notice significant changes in weight… you may need several months"
  ([Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/drugs/not-losing-weight-on-zepbound)).
- **The down-titration mirror:** "I lowered my dose because of side effects, and the cravings started
  to sneak back" ([medino aggregation](https://www.medino.com/article/real-stories-about-mounjaro-and-food-noise)).
- The lived diary version: Krause reached 15 mg at month 5 and immediately re-ran the whole
  side-effect adjustment ([Krause](https://stevendkrause.substack.com/p/zepbound-starting-month-5)).

## 4. The recurring anxieties (ranked by how often they surface)

1. **Stall/plateau while paying for the medication.** The signature GLP-1 anxiety: weight flat 2-3
   weeks → "is it still working?" → dose-increase question. Whole podcast episodes exist for it
   ([On The Pen Eps 106, "Zepbound Weight Loss Stalls"](https://onthepen.substack.com/p/zepbound-weight-loss-stalls-tips-0d4);
   ["Does Zepbound Stop Working?"](https://onthepen.substack.com/p/does-zepbound-stop-working)).
   Clinical framing: plateaus are normal, most trial effects are measured at 52-72 weeks
   ([MedicalNewsToday](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/drugs-zepbound-not-working)).
2. **"The food noise is back — is the medication failing?"** Conflates the normal day-5-7 trough with
   tolerance. The trough is weekly and expected; the person experiences it as a verdict. "Even just a
   little food noise feels dangerous now" ([medino](https://www.medino.com/article/real-stories-about-mounjaro-and-food-noise)).
3. **Muscle/protein worry.** Widely publicized: older adults and women lose more lean mass on
   semaglutide; adequate protein + resistance training is protective (Haines/MGH:
   "older adults and women may be more likely to lose muscle on semaglutide, but eating more protein
   may help protect against this" —
   [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health-news/protein-muscle-loss-weight-loss-drugs);
   2025 case series: protein 0.36-0.77 g/lb + training 3-5×/wk preserved or gained lean mass despite
   13-33% total loss). The daily question it produces: "did I get enough protein TODAY?"
4. **Side-effect pattern questions.** Constipation (slow onset day 3-7, lingers), nausea (day 1-2),
   fatigue (day 1-3), sulfur burps, and the newer "Wegovy itch" at higher doses
   ([Bloomberg 2025-09-15](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2025-09-15/wegovy-itch-emerges-as-new-side-effect-as-doctors-boost-dosage)).
   The lived question is comparative: "is this normal FOR ME / worse than last week?"
5. **Injection-site issues.** Lumps/nodules, bruising, itching days later; when is it a reaction vs
   normal ([Klarity](https://www.helloklarity.com/post/glp-1-injection-site-reactions-whats-normal-and-when-to-call-your-doctor/),
   [SkinnyRx](https://skinnyrx.com/blog/glp-1-injection-site-reactions)). "Which site did I use when
   that lump appeared?" is a record question.
6. **Missed/late doses** (windows above) — plus the derived fears: "did missing a day cause this
   week's hunger?", "will one miss undo my progress?" ("a single lapse rarely harms… but repeated
   misses weaken appetite control" — [Eureka Health](https://www.eurekahealth.com/resources/missed-your-glp-1-dose-en)).
7. **Travel/supply logistics.** Mounjaro/Zepbound pens: **21 days at room temp ≤86°F** (30 for
   KwikPen/vials), then discard; fridge 36-46°F; TSA allows medically necessary liquids + cold packs
   ([MedicalNewsToday](https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/drugs-mounjaro-travel),
   [GoodRx](https://www.goodrx.com/mounjaro/how-to-store)). The lived questions: "when did THIS pen
   leave the fridge?", "do I have enough doses to cover the trip?", "can I shift my shot day across
   time zones?"
8. **Access/refill anxiety** — the #4 theme in the published subreddit analysis (10.7% of posts:
   shortages, insurance, cost — [Plenn et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12327412/)).
   Mostly outside an app's power, except "how many doses left in the pen / days until I need a refill."

## 5. The companion-app landscape: what's answered, what's complained about

- **Shotsy** (most-downloaded, ~1M): excellent at the mechanical layer — dose logging, reminders,
  estimated medication-level (PK) chart, weight trend; v3.0 (Mar 2026) added oral-pill tracking and a
  Maintenance Mode. The 2026 criticism is blunt: "zero nutrition features, no food database, no meal
  logging, no calorie or protein tracking… doesn't track nutrition, body composition, protein intake,
  or muscle loss — the factors research identifies as primary determinants of long-term outcomes"
  ([Metabolic Health Today review](https://www.glp1muscleloss.com/articles/shotsy-review/)); users
  also complain the weight graph's Total Change/Weekly Avg can't scope to 1-3 month windows
  ([Google Play reviews](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.shotsy.app&hl=en_US)).
- **MeAgain** (4.8★): broadest all-in-one (AI food logging, medication-level widget, Watch); review
  mining surfaces glitch complaints (barcode/meal adds failing, can't change shot day even on
  premium), a requested "correction button for scanned foods due to macro accuracy issues", and
  pay-before-you-see friction ([justuseapp review mining](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/6744178534/meagain-glp-1-all-in-one-app/reviews)).
- **Glapp** (launched Mar 2026, $4.99): "medication-cycle interpretation and supply tracking" — an
  estimated level view compared against clinical-trial data; called "best for shot-cycle insights"
  ([Health Review Network](https://healthreviewnetwork.com/weight-loss/glapp-glp-1-tracker-review/),
  [Regimen comparison](https://helloregimen.com/blog/best-glp1-tracker-apps-2026)). Validates that
  the dose-cycle-as-insight seat is being contested RIGHT NOW.
- **GlucoPal**: the one competitor praised for "symptom history timeline tied to dose schedule —
  uniquely visualizes side effect patterns relative to injection timing"
  ([gila.coach 8-app test](https://gila.coach/learn/best-glp1-tracking-apps-compared-2026)).
- **The category-wide gap**, per the same 8-app test: "almost none of them address what happens after
  the tracker" — logging without interpretation, no correlation of side effects with the individual's
  own dose pattern (beyond GlucoPal), no adaptation to medication phase, no help building the
  routines that keep people on therapy through year one.

Net: the market answers "log the shot, remind the shot, draw the weight line." It largely does NOT
answer "what does MY week look like," "is this symptom on schedule FOR ME," "what happened the last
time my dose changed," or the protein/muscle question at all.

## 6. Answerable from her own record vs must-refuse

**Answerable from a longitudinal record (doses + symptoms + food + weight in one place):**
- Which day of MY dose week is today, and what has that day looked like for me before (appetite,
  symptoms, protein logged, kcal pattern).
- When did I last inject and where; which sites I've used recently.
- What happened after my LAST dose change (side-effect duration, appetite, weight movement in the
  window) — stated as her own history.
- Whether this week's hunger is the recurring end-of-week pattern in her record vs unusual for her.
- Whether the trend (not the scale) moved over the window; whether a "stall" is within her record's
  normal week-to-week noise.
- Protein logged vs her floor, on the suppressed days when she won't feel hunger cues.
- Pen arithmetic: doses remaining by her own count, run-out date on a fixed rhythm, days a pen has
  been out of the fridge IF she stated the event.
- Whether she logged the dose late/missed, and what her record shows followed — as timeline fact.

**Must refuse / route to the prescriber (an app that answers these is practicing medicine):**
- "Should I take the missed dose now or skip?" — window rules are label facts an app may QUOTE with
  attribution, but the decision belongs to the label + clinician; never compute a personalized verdict.
- "Should I go up / stay / split my dose?" — never.
- "Is this side effect dangerous?" — describe-and-route only (severe pain, persistent vomiting,
  allergic-reaction signs are call-your-doctor territory per every clinical source).
- "Is the medication still working for me?" — the app can show the observed trend and the record;
  the judgment is clinical.
- Any timing→causality claim ("the shot caused the stall," "day-3 constipation is the dose") — the
  record shows co-occurrence in HER timeline; causality is never stated (Jeni's standing
  timing-never-causality law matches the evidence boundary exactly).

## What this means for Jeni

Jeni already holds the pieces no competitor combines: `CyclePosition` (event-anchored day 1..7 with
the waning band), `DoseLedger` + sites, `SymptomLedger`, the one weight fold, the protein floor, food
logs with authorship, `PenSupply`, and `foodNoiseReturn`. Ranked moment-anchored questions Jeni could
answer from its own record (strength: STRONG = published data + repeated firsthand accounts; MODERATE
= repeated community reports; WEAK = thin/secondhand):

1. **Day 5-7, the waning: "why am I so hungry — is it failing?"** → "day 6 of your dose week. the
   hungry end. your record shows this week's shape before." STRONG (PK curve + the most-quoted
   community pattern). Constraint: state HER recorded pattern and the cycle day; never promise the
   next shot fixes it; never call it tolerance or failure.
2. **Shot-day morning: "where did I inject last / when / which site?"** → the ledger answers
   directly; rotation SUGGESTED never insisted (already law). STRONG. Constraint: no site-efficacy
   claims (evidence says sites are equivalent).
3. **The 2-3 week "stall" panic: "is it still working?"** → the trend fold + her own week-to-week
   variance: "this week reads flat. the month is still down 4.1 lb." (p74's steadyContext is exactly
   this answer). STRONG. Constraint: observed rate only, never a projection, never a verdict on the
   medication.
4. **Post-titration watch (weeks 1-4 on a new dose): "is this nausea normal? how long did it last
   before?"** → the era ledger + her symptom timeline from the LAST change: "after your last dose
   change, queasy days showed up for 9 days, then quieted, by your record." MODERATE-STRONG
   (titration side-effect reset is well documented; per-person comparability is the record's unique
   value). Constraint: timing ≠ causality wording; young era (<4 wks) never gets a rate (already law).
5. **Peak-suppression days 1-3: "I'm not hungry at all — protein?"** → lead with protein-to-floor on
   the days her record shows lowest intake; the muscle-preservation evidence is the strongest
   nutrition science in the domain. STRONG. Constraint: never grade; absence of appetite is the drug,
   not virtue.
6. **The late/missed dose moment: "I'm a day late — what happened last time?"** → record the true
   day (backfill + late face already exist), show the label window AS A QUOTED FACT with routing, and
   answer "your record: you logged it saturday; the next slot moved." MODERATE. Constraint: never
   compute take-vs-skip; quote-and-route only.
7. **Day 2-4 symptom check: "is this constipation on my usual schedule?"** → the pattern composer's
   dose↔symptom co-occurrence, phrased as her timeline ("constipation has landed 2-4 days after your
   shot in 3 of your last 4 weeks"). MODERATE (clinical onset windows + GlucoPal proves demand).
   Constraint: co-occurrence language only; severe-symptom routing.
8. **Trip planning: "do my doses cover the trip? when did this pen leave the fridge?"** → PenSupply
   run-out date already ships; a stated fridge-out event would complete it. MODERATE (recurring
   logistics thread). Constraint: storage limits quoted from the label per product, never computed
   as advice.
9. **The dose-day bookkeeping ritual:** shot day is the community's natural weigh-in/review day —
   the regimen page's daily read (p78) can carry the week's receipt on dose day. MODERATE.
10. **Oral cohort's morning sequence** (empty stomach, 30-min wait, other-pill ordering): a daily
    checklist moment Jeni could acknowledge for oral regimens. WEAK-MODERATE for community pain
    volume, STRONG for the label facts. Constraint: sequencing of OTHER medications is prescriber
    territory; quote the Rybelsus label only.

The single sharpest product implication: the category's apps draw an ESTIMATED drug-level curve from
population PK; Jeni's differentiated move is the opposite — speak the person's OWN recorded week back
to her at the exact moment she's living it, with the cycle day as the anchor and her history as the
only evidence. That is the question the market demonstrably isn't answering, and it's the one Jeni's
record was built to answer.
