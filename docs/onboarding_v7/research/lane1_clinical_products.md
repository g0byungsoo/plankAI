# Lane 1 — Clinical-Credibility Health Products: Onboarding Principles

Research lane: Noom, Ro Body, Calibrate, Found, Omada, Sequence/WW Clinic,
Hims/Hers, Virta. Extracted principles only, no flows to copy. Each
principle: claim, evidence, one-line translation to a quiz-style iOS
onboarding. (Agent-produced 2026-08-02; URLs verified by the agent.)

---

## A. Establishing clinical credibility during intake

**P1. Open as an eligibility screen, not a sales quiz, and be willing to say no.**
Calibrate starts with a ~2-minute eligibility quiz whose answers are "later validated by labs and a video doctor visit" ([joincalibrate.com/pages/calibrate-eligibility](https://www.joincalibrate.com/pages/calibrate-eligibility)); Omada runs a two-step coverage check then a "clinical eligibility" application, after which you are "accepted" and paired with a coach ([support.omadahealth.com](https://support.omadahealth.com/hc/en-us/articles/4412324057619-What-do-I-need-to-do-to-join-Omada)); WW Clinic publishes hard exclusions (BMI floors, pregnancy, active cancer, anorexia/bulimia history) ([rxweightlossguide.com](https://www.rxweightlossguide.com/reviews/sequence-weight-loss-review)). The willingness to decline is itself the credibility signal.
*Translate: make one early beat a genuine screen with a real decline/redirect path; "you qualified" then carries earned weight.*

**P2. Give a one-line clinical "why we ask" before every sensitive question.**
Noom precedes its sex/hormones question with "Sex and hormones impact how our bodies metabolize food" and softens intrusion with "We don't mean to pry, we just need this to build a plan that's right for you" ([revenuecat.com teardown](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/web-to-app-onboarding-funnel/)). The Behavioral Scientist critique notes rationale-free sensitive questions read as data harvesting ([thebehavioralscientist.com](https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/articles/noom-product-critique-onboarding)).
*Translate: a small ink sub-line above weight/medication/cycle asks stating the mechanism the answer feeds; number+unit+basis register extends to "question+reason".*

**P3. Safety screening must be able to change or end the flow, not decorate it.**
Noom blocks underweight goal entry and blocks continuation on pregnancy without a waiver; its eating-disorder question gates with a waiver (critiqued as too skippable) ([revenuecat.com](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/web-to-app-onboarding-funnel/)). The counterexample is load-bearing: Noom was publicly attacked because its early survey didn't screen ED history while its marketing courted recovery-community language ([femestella.com](https://www.femestella.com/noom-reviews-horror-stories-eating-disorders/), [psychologytoday.com](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/eating-mindfully/202005/is-noom-diet)).
*Translate: keep the safety gate pre-paywall and let a flagged answer visibly alter the plan (or refuse to sell); refusal converts to trust.*

**P4. Put a named, credentialed human near the medical moments.**
Ro surfaces "Dr. Steve Silvestro, MD, DABOM" and repeats "Ro-affiliated provider" language, with intake "reviewed by a licensed provider" ([ro.co/weight-loss](https://ro.co/weight-loss/)); WW Clinic has a board-certified clinician review intake asynchronously ([rxweightlossguide.com](https://www.rxweightlossguide.com/reviews/sequence-weight-loss-review)); Found states "physicians review every case" ([finvsfin.com](https://finvsfin.com/found-weight-loss-review/)).
*Translate: a non-telehealth app can truthfully say "designed against ACSM/clinical guidelines" or show a named advisor only if real; never simulate clinician review (data-provenance law).*

**P5. Cite evidence like a paper, not a poster.**
Virta footnotes claims to named authors and journals (Hallberg SJ, McKenzie AL et al., *Diabetes Therapy*) and qualifies each stat ("one-year completers", "excludes metformin", "aggregated de-identified data") ([virtahealth.com/how-it-works](https://www.virtahealth.com/how-it-works)); Omada leans on ~30 peer-reviewed publications and full CDC recognition rather than adjectives ([omadahealth.com](https://www.omadahealth.com/resource-center/omada-health-achieves-full-cdc-recognition)); Calibrate links a downloadable "clinically-reviewed Results Report" behind its 16/18/20% curve ([joincalibrate.com/how-it-works](https://www.joincalibrate.com/how-it-works)).
*Translate: every on-screen number carries source + population + timeframe in small print; one named real source beats three vague "studies show".*

**P6. Qualify outcome statistics with n, denominator, and the honest comparator.**
Ro: "87% have seen life-changing results" is footnoted "survey results of 1,243 Ro members taking GLP-1 medication for at least 7 weeks", and med outcomes are attributed to "manufacturer trials studying the highest doses", always against "~2-4% with diet and exercise alone" ([ro.co/weight-loss](https://ro.co/weight-loss/)). Omada's modest 4-5% average is noted by reviewers as *supported*, which reads better than inflated claims ([millennialhawk.com](https://millennialhawk.com/omada-health-review/)). Noom's "78% lost weight" is critiqued as company-funded and slippery ([thebehavioralscientist.com](https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/articles/noom-product-critique-onboarding)).
*Translate: prefer a modest number with a basis over an impressive orphan number; the comparator ("vs. dieting alone") is where persuasion legitimately lives.*

## B. Quiz intelligence: personalization done credibly

**P7. Every clinical disclosure must produce a visible downstream consequence.**
Noom's diabetes answer triggers two tailored follow-ups; reviewers explicitly flagged the one branch (dietary restrictions) that did NOT follow up as a broken promise, because conditional logic had been pattern-set ([revenuecat.com](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/web-to-app-onboarding-funnel/)).
*Translate: if she says GLP-1-current, the next screen must prove it heard her; an unacknowledged disclosure damages more than not asking.*

**P8. Acknowledge vulnerable inputs explicitly, and calibrate empathy to severity.**
Noom after weight entry: "Thank you for sharing. That's an important (and hard) first step."; after a condition disclosure: "We're really glad you shared. Noom's mission is helping people get healthier, whatever that is for them." ([growthwaves.io](https://www.growthwaves.io/p/the-113-screen-onboarding-that-doesnt)). RevenueCat notes "selective empathy": some answers get reassurance screens, others deliberately don't, which keeps warmth from becoming wallpaper. Escape hatches ("I haven't decided" goal option, age bands instead of exact age, unit toggles) reduce load without losing signal.
*Translate: short acknowledgment beats after weight, medication, and fear statements only; identical boilerplate after every answer reads as automation.*

**P9. Reciprocity law: never ask twice without giving once.**
The 113-screen flow doesn't feel long because nearly every input returns acknowledgment, education, or an updated projection; "Length isn't the problem; emptiness is... Greediness is asking and asking without giving and guiding" ([growthwaves.io](https://www.growthwaves.io/p/the-113-screen-onboarding-that-doesnt)). Noom's goal-completion date visibly moves closer as answers accumulate, rewarding effort ([revenuecat.com](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/web-to-app-onboarding-funnel/)).
*Translate: recompute and re-render her projection artifact at act boundaries so answering feels like building, not surrendering data.*

**P10. Teach the method inside the quiz so the paywall has no unanswered "what actually is it".**
Noom's food questions double as calorie-density education; "by the time you finish, you already understand their method" ([revenuecat.com](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/web-to-app-onboarding-funnel/)). Critique: the core "no bad foods" philosophy was buried late and should surface early ([thebehavioralscientist.com](https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/articles/noom-product-critique-onboarding)).
*Translate: 2-3 interstitials that each teach ONE true mechanism tied to her previous answer; lead with the philosophy, don't save it for the reveal.*

**P11. Ask the fork-in-the-road question early; skip the ten that change nothing.**
Kristen Berman on GLP-1 startups (Ro, Hims, Noom): "Ask the one question that changes the journey, not ten that change nothing... ask the fork-in-the-road question early and every screen after it can be the right one"; Ro forks on insurance, Hims on medication intent ([kristenberman.substack.com](https://kristenberman.substack.com/p/want-users-to-stick-around-these)).
*Translate: GLP-1 status at the top of Act II is exactly this pattern (already shipped); audit remaining questions for "does this change any screen after it".*

**P12. Loaders are operational transparency, and they only work when the steps are real.**
The Behavioral Scientist praises Noom's analysis loaders via Buell/Norton's operational-transparency research; web2appworld: "a 10-15 second computing-your-results animation makes the output feel personalized and valuable" ([web2appworld.com](https://web2appworld.com/breakdowns/noom/)). RevenueCat catches the failure mode: Noom's loader justification text was dated 2021 while running in 2026, which "undermines the transparency narrative".
*Translate: loader lines name only truly computed keys (the receipt-tape loader's live-keys-only rule is this principle as law).*

**P13. Name the assessment engine, but only if it maps to real computation.**
Found brands its intake "MetabolicPrint", an obesity-phenotype assessment, and grounds it in a real 2021 *Obesity* study (phenotype-guided care: ~16% vs 9% loss) ([joinfound.com](https://joinfound.com/blog/best-weight-loss-drugs)); Virta trademarks "The Virta Method" ([virtahealth.com](https://www.virtahealth.com/how-it-works)).
*Translate: a named, one-time "assessment" artifact (her file, her basis) raises perceived intelligence; naming vapor lowers it permanently.*

## C. Problem framing: the persuasion architecture

**P14. Biology-not-willpower is the master frame; make the enemy the old method, never her.**
Calibrate: "GLP-1s address the underlying biology that is most important for weight loss, and help lower your set point, or the weight your body fights to stay at" ([joincalibrate.com/how-it-works](https://www.joincalibrate.com/how-it-works)). Found: "weight loss is NOT all about willpower", then names mechanisms (gut microbes that "cause you to crave sugar", cortisol from short sleep) ([finvsfin.com](https://finvsfin.com/found-weight-loss-review/)). Noom's projection graph contrasts a steady line against the yo-yo crash-diet curve, deliberately framing "restrictive dieting", not other apps, as the competitor ([revenuecat.com](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/web-to-app-onboarding-funnel/)).
*Translate: one interstitial that explains, from her own history answers, why the last attempts stalled (set point, adaptation), with a named source.*

**P15. Absorb her past failures as diagnostic data, not fault.**
Found's quiz opens with "what you tried in the past to lose weight, why you're motivated, and how long you've been trying" ([finvsfin.com](https://finvsfin.com/found-weight-loss-review/)); Noom stays "positive and non-judgmental" even on wrong quiz answers ([revenuecat.com](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/web-to-app-onboarding-funnel/)).
*Translate: "you've tried X, that's evidence, not failure" acknowledgment converts shame into plan-justifying input.*

**P16. Position the product as root-cause care, not tracking.**
Virta sells "reversal" against mere management ("Eat real food, get real results"; deprescription framed as the outcome: "Fewer diabetes medications allows members to reimagine their future") ([virtahealth.com/how-it-works](https://www.virtahealth.com/how-it-works)); Calibrate: "(MUCH) MORE THAN MEDICATION"; Ro sells membership-as-care ("regular check-ins, expert guidance, and access to your care team 24/7") ([ro.co/weight-loss](https://ro.co/weight-loss/)).
*Translate: the reveal should read as a care plan with a mechanism and phases, not a feature list.*

**P17. Anchor honest expectations early and repeat them; realism outsells hype.**
Noom repeats "typically lose 0.5-1 kg per week" and refuses to promise the full goal by a near event date; RevenueCat: "That realism builds more trust than aggressive promises ever could" ([revenuecat.com](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/web-to-app-onboarding-funnel/)).
*Translate: the projection curve must respect pace floors and say so out loud ("we won't show you a faster line than is safe").*

## D. The payment moment

**P18. Sell a program with a named shape and term, not a subscription with a price.**
Calibrate: "One-Year Metabolic Reset", $1,620 billed once or $135/month via Affirm, with a phased curriculum timeline ([joincalibrate.com](https://www.joincalibrate.com/lp/pricing1)); Hims converts intake into prepaid 3- and 5-month "treatment plans" ([thedigitalchapter.com](https://www.thedigitalchapter.com/p/how-hims-and-hers-scaled-to-1-46b)); Ro prices the first month ($45) as a medical service covering intake review and labs ([ro.co/weight-loss](https://ro.co/weight-loss/)).
*Translate: name the term ("your first 12 weeks"), show what each phase contains, then price the whole; the wall sells the program object, the store sells the subscription.*

**P19. Deliver the personalized verdict BEFORE asking for money.**
WW Clinic gives a free eligibility determination "before providing a payment method" ([rxweightlossguide.com](https://www.rxweightlossguide.com/reviews/sequence-weight-loss-review)); Noom shows the full results screen (goal date, event marker, realistic curve) before price, and its email gate CTA is benefit-framed: "See my results" ([revenuecat.com](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/web-to-app-onboarding-funnel/)). RevenueCat's takeaway #11: "Build commitment before introducing price."
*Translate: the complete personalized read (curve, plan, first week) is free; only continuation is paid; never gate the verdict itself.*

**P20. Show the actual product before the buy button; the biggest documented gap in the category.**
RevenueCat's sharpest critique of Noom: "After all of this, I still don't know what the app experience actually looks like", no food-logging UI, no daily-lesson visual, listed as a conversion-confidence failure ([revenuecat.com](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/web-to-app-onboarding-funnel/)).
*Translate: the real Day-1 mock as first-week preview is exactly the fix; keep it product-true, never a stylized fake.*

**P21. Stack earned trust below the decision: guarantee with one-sentence terms, third-party seals, real reviews.**
Calibrate places its Results Money Back Promise (50% refund if under 10% loss at 12 months, with conditions) plus LegitScript certification near purchase ([joincalibrate.com/legal/guarantee](https://www.joincalibrate.com/legal/guarantee)); Found offers a 90-day money-back guarantee ([finvsfin.com](https://finvsfin.com/found-weight-loss-review/)). Counterweight: Noom's guarantee conditioned on "completing the program" was critiqued as accountability-shifting with buried terms ([thebehavioralscientist.com](https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/articles/noom-product-critique-onboarding)).
*Translate: only offer a guarantee whose conditions fit in one honest sentence beside the button; otherwise offer none (a dormant real-proof band beats a fake promise).*

**P22. One total, billed today, no surprise splits.**
The most repeated complaint against WW Clinic and Ro is two-part billing (membership + separate pharmacy bill): members "budget based on the advertised membership rate and are surprised by the total" ([rxweightlossguide.com](https://www.rxweightlossguide.com/reviews/sequence-weight-loss-review), [telehealthally.com](https://telehealthally.com/reviews/weightwatchers-clinic-review)).
*Translate: billed-today everywhere, per-week equivalents subordinate (already law); never decompose price into parts that obscure the total.*

## E. Voice and register

**P23. The register is plain declaratives + qualified numbers, with warmth event-triggered at vulnerable moments only.**
Quote bank of lines that hit "calm, confident, evidence-based, warm but not cute":
- "Thank you for sharing. That's an important (and hard) first step." (Noom, post-weight-entry)
- "We don't mean to pry, we just need this to build a plan that's right for you." (Noom)
- "Sex and hormones impact how our bodies metabolize food." (Noom, rationale line)
- "There's room for it. Just be mindful." (Noom, food philosophy)
- "GLP-1s... help lower your set point, or the weight your body fights to stay at." (Calibrate, mechanism in plain speech)
- "Eat real food, get real results." / "Powered by technology, delivered by humans." (Virta)
- "See if you qualify" (Ro CTA: eligibility, not purchase)
- "Weight loss is NOT all about willpower." (Found)
Sources: [revenuecat.com](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/web-to-app-onboarding-funnel/), [growthwaves.io](https://www.growthwaves.io/p/the-113-screen-onboarding-that-doesnt), [joincalibrate.com](https://www.joincalibrate.com/how-it-works), [virtahealth.com](https://www.virtahealth.com/how-it-works), [ro.co](https://ro.co/weight-loss/), [finvsfin.com](https://finvsfin.com/found-weight-loss-review/).
*Translate: warmth fires after disclosures; mechanism lines carry the confidence; CTAs frame qualification and continuation, not buying.*

## F. Regulatory and compliance floors

**P24. FTC floors: RCT-grade substantiation for outcome claims, and they apply to apps.**
The 2022 Health Products Compliance Guidance requires "competent and reliable scientific evidence", generally randomized controlled human trials, for health benefit claims, explicitly including health-related apps ([ftc.gov guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance), [cooley.com](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2023/2023-03-02-ftc-revises-health-products-compliance-guidance)). The "Gut Check" list flags facially false weight claims, and endorsement/testimonial rules apply to ALL weight-loss programs ([ftc.gov gut check](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/gut-check-reference-guide-media-spotting-false-weight-loss-claims)). Subscription mechanics: Noom's settlement required a separate affirmative action (checkbox/signature) to accept autorenewal, and despite the Eighth Circuit vacating click-to-cancel, the FTC keeps enforcing cancel-as-easy-as-signup under ROSCA and is re-running the rulemaking ([kelleydrye.com](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/noom-to-pay-over-60m-to-cancel-automatic-renewal-suit), [hklaw.com](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/09/ftc-steps-up-subscription-enforcement-after-click-to-cancel-rule)).
*Translate: no first-party numeric weight-loss claims without cited third-party evidence and population; separate, explicit renewal consent; cancellation parity.*

**P25. Drug-name and drug-adjacency floors: class names, not brands; never advertise outcomes without risk context.**
Apps that reference Ozempic/Wegovy survive review only with ® marks and non-affiliation disclaimers under Apple 5.2 IP rules; the safe register is class language ("GLP-1 medications") ([developer.apple.com guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). The cautionary tale: Hims' Super Bowl ad drew a senators' letter to the FDA for "omitting safety or side effect information", with "not FDA-approved" shown three seconds in microtype, plus industry condemnation over compounded semaglutide ([cnbc.com](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/02/07/hims-hers-faces-lawmaker-scrutiny-over-misleading-super-bowl-ad-.html), [fiercepharma.com](https://www.fiercepharma.com/marketing/novo-nordisk-calls-out-dangers-compounded-glp-1s-following-hims-hers-super-bowl-ad)). Ro, by contrast, prints serious side-effect warnings ("possible thyroid tumors") on its own marketing page, which reviewers read as credibility ([ro.co/weight-loss](https://ro.co/weight-loss/)).
*Translate: brand-name ban stays absolute; on-medication branches speak in class terms; where medication outcomes are referenced, risk acknowledgment is a trust asset, not a legal tax.*

## Anti-patterns (documented failures to avoid)

1. **Cancel asymmetry.** Easy signup + coach-mediated cancellation + vague autorenew disclosure cost Noom $62M and a court-ordered redesign ([hunton.com](https://www.hunton.com/hunton-retail-law-resource/fitness-app-agrees-to-pay-56-million-to-settle-class-action-alleging-dark-pattern-practices)).
2. **Fake urgency.** Noom's 15-minute countdown for a "free" bonus course; critiqued as "pressure without demonstrating actual value" ([thebehavioralscientist.com](https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/articles/noom-product-critique-onboarding)).
3. **Choice theater.** "Pay what you want" trial tiers that all lead to the same offer; reads as contribution framing until noticed, then as manipulation ([revenuecat.com](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/web-to-app-onboarding-funnel/)).
4. **Stale evidence.** Stats and cost-justifications dated 2021 still rendering in 2026 "undermines the transparency narrative" ([revenuecat.com](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/web-to-app-onboarding-funnel/)).
5. **Register incongruence.** Marketing "anti-diet" language over a 1,200-kcal stoplight diet triggered sustained backlash and ED-community accusations; the register you claim must match the product's mechanics ([outsideonline.com](https://www.outsideonline.com/health/nutrition/noom-app-diet-trend/), [bust.com](https://bust.com/the-toxic-diet-app-noom-is-finally-being-seen-for-what-it-really-is/)).
6. **Premature intimacy.** Asking for accountability partners and cross-selling stress add-ons mid-intake, before any product experience, "feels manipulative and presumptuous" ([thebehavioralscientist.com](https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/articles/noom-product-critique-onboarding)).
7. **Orphan comparatives.** "Lose twice as much weight with Noom" and "32% faster" with no verifiable basis actively erode the credible numbers nearby ([thebehavioralscientist.com](https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/articles/noom-product-critique-onboarding)).
8. **Medical-looking questions that only serve segmentation.** The intake "conflates health assessment with sales optimization"; if a clinical-feeling question never changes the plan, users eventually feel it ([thebehavioralscientist.com](https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/articles/noom-product-critique-onboarding)).
9. **Split pricing surprise.** Advertised membership price + separate medication bill = the dominant complaint against WW Clinic/Ro ([rxweightlossguide.com](https://www.rxweightlossguide.com/reviews/sequence-weight-loss-review)).
10. **Risk-free drug adjacency.** Promoting medication outcomes while burying "not FDA-approved" and omitting side effects drew senators, the FDA, and safety groups onto Hims ([cnbc.com](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/02/07/hims-hers-faces-lawmaker-scrutiny-over-misleading-super-bowl-ad-.html)).
11. **Guarantee with buried conditions.** "Money back if you complete the program" with undisclosed completion criteria shifts blame to the user and reads as a trap once the terms surface ([thebehavioralscientist.com](https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/articles/noom-product-critique-onboarding), [joincalibrate.com/legal/guarantee](https://www.joincalibrate.com/legal/guarantee) for the disclosed-conditions contrast).

## One meta-observation

The category's credibility ceiling is set by the *cheapest thing on the
screen*: one fake timer, one orphan statistic, or one unacknowledged
disclosure re-frames every legitimate clinical element around it as
costume. The products that survive scrutiny (Virta, Omada, Calibrate's
evidence surfaces) all share one property: every persuasive element is
also literally true and traceable, which is already Jeni's
data-provenance law applied to marketing surfaces.
