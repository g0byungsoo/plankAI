# What honest step-insights can we author from a phone? (2026-07-06, verified)

Question: JeniFit holds phone-sourced step data (HealthKit, iPhone-only
for most users, no watch assumed; current anchor 7,500). Founder
principle: "create value even when the user does very little." Steps are
the most passive signal we hold. So: what honest, useful, low-effort
insights can we derive for weight management in women 22-35, and what
does the evidence actually support?

Method: 5 parallel search-and-extract agents (dose-response · step
patterns + NEAT · GLP-1 + interventions · phone-vs-watch accuracy ·
product-insight teardown) → ~80 claims → 3-vote adversarial verification
on the 14 load-bearing / flagged claims. Confidence tags: **[strong]**
multiple primary sources + survived verification · **[moderate]** single
primary or acute-lab / small-n · **[weak]** inferential or descriptive
only · **[product-fact]** company/press-verified product behavior.
Verification status noted where a claim was corrected or killed.

Bottom line up front: **the strongest step evidence is in OLDER adults;
the step-to-adiposity evidence specific to young women is weaker, mostly
cross-sectional, and one large study found no significant association in
women at all.** Steps are a real health signal and a legitimate passive
value source, but they are an adjunct to eating change, not a weight-loss
engine. Almost everything defensible is an *association*. Our 7,500 anchor
is well-supported. Design the copy around trends, personal baselines, and
a gentle floor, never around a round-number target or a scale promise.

## LANE 1 — the evidence

### 1. Steps to mortality / health: the dose-response and the anchors

**Lee 2019 — the women-specific plateau [strong, CONFIRMED]**
Lee IM et al., JAMA Internal Medicine 2019. Women's Health Study,
N=16,741 women, mean age **72.0** (SD 5.7), 4.3-yr follow-up, 504 deaths.
Mortality fell progressively with more steps until **~7,500 steps/day,
then leveled off**. Quartiles (median 2,718 / 4,363 / 5,905 / 8,442
steps): adjusted HR 1.00 / 0.59 (0.47-0.75) / 0.54 (0.41-0.72) / **0.42
(0.30-0.60)**. Stepping *intensity* (cadence) was NOT associated with
mortality after adjusting for total daily steps.
→ Volume matters more than speed. This is the origin of our 7,500 anchor.
FLAG: elderly women; read-across to 22-35 is directional, not evidenced.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6547157/

**Paluch 2022 — the age-split plateau [strong, CONFIRMED via Ding-check]**
Paluch AE et al., Lancet Public Health 2022 (Steps for Health
Collaborative). 15 cohorts, N=47,471, mean age 65, 68% female,
device-measured. Mortality plateaus at **~6,000-8,000 steps/day for
adults ≥60** and **~8,000-10,000 for adults <60**. Quartile HRs vs
lowest: 0.60 / 0.55 / 0.47. No significant sex interaction.
→ The <60 stratum (most relevant to us) plateaus higher, but the cohort
mean age is still 65. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9289978/

**Banach 2023 — the per-1,000-step gradient [strong, from search]**
Banach M et al., European Journal of Preventive Cardiology 2023. 17
cohorts, N=226,889, mean age 64, 49% female. Each **+1,000 steps/day →
15% lower all-cause mortality** (HR 0.85, 0.81-0.91); +500 → 7% lower CV
mortality. Benefit detectable from as few as **~3,900 steps** (all-cause)
and ~2,300 (CV). No significant sex difference.
→ There is no cliff. The curve is smooth and starts low.
https://academic.oup.com/eurjpc/article/30/18/1975/7226309

**Ding 2025 — the newest, largest; "7,000 is the real target" [strong, CONFIRMED]**
Ding D et al., Lancet Public Health 2025 (published 2025-07-23). 57
studies from 35 cohorts. **7,000 vs 2,000 steps/day → 47% lower all-cause
mortality (HR 0.53, 95% CI 0.46-0.60)**, plus lower CVD, T2D, cancer,
dementia, depressive symptoms, and falls. Inflection points ~5,000-7,000
for several outcomes. Authors explicitly propose **~7,000 steps/day as a
"more realistic and achievable target"** than 10,000.
→ The field is moving our way: 7,000-7,500 is now the evidence-backed
anchor, and 10,000 is being retired as a default. Covers mortality/disease,
NOT body weight directly. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40713949/

**The 10,000-step goal is marketing, not science [strong, CONFIRMED]**
"Manpo-kei" (万歩計, "10,000-steps meter"), a pedometer sold by Yamasa
around the 1964 Tokyo Olympics. Harvard's I-Min Lee: there were no studies
behind 10,000; it "was a made-up number," partly chosen because the kanji
万 resembles a walking figure.
→ We can honestly tell users the round number they've been shamed by was
an ad. https://www.popsci.com/health/10000-steps-debunk-science/

### 2. Steps to body weight / adiposity — the core angle, and it's weaker

**Women-specific adiposity review [moderate, CONFIRMED]**
"Objectively determined physical activity and adiposity measures in adult
women: a systematic review and meta-analysis," PMC9445154 (2022). 35
studies, N=9,176 women, ages 18-64 (11 studies in 18-39). Cross-sectional
inverse associations: **BMI r=−0.32; body-fat% r=−0.41; fat mass r=−0.36;
waist r=−0.27**. Every +1,000 steps/day ≈ 2.4% lower body-fat%; women
≥10,000 steps/day had large reductions in overweight/obesity odds.
BUT — the load-bearing caveat — when restricted to walking **INTERVENTION**
studies, the pooled effect was significant **only for waist circumference
(SMD −0.35)** and **NOT** for BMI, body-fat%, or visceral fat. Authors
rate overall evidence quality LOW.
→ The correlation (leaner women walk more) is much stronger than the
causal lever (walking more makes you leaner). Reverse causation is likely.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9445154/

**The null-in-women counterexample [moderate, from search — cite for balance]**
ELANS (Latin America), PMC8123766, N=2,524, ages 18-65. Steps were
inversely associated with BMI and waist overall and in men, but showed
**NO significant association with body composition in women**.
→ Do not tell a woman "more steps = leaner you" as fact. It is not
established in our demographic. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8123766/

**Step-Up trial — thresholds for active loss [moderate, from search]**
Creasy SA et al., PMC5970037. N=260, mean age 43, **79% women**, BMI 33.
Those achieving ≥10% loss at 18 mo averaged ~9,800 steps/day incl. ~3,500
as bout-MVPA, vs 7,800 (only ~1,100 MVPA) in weight-gainers. Each +1,000
total steps → 0.21 kg extra loss; each +1,000 MVPA steps → 0.33 kg.
→ For *active* loss, some brisk walking (not just volume) helps. Best
audience match of the trials found (79% women), but step level wasn't
randomized, so still association. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5970037/

**El Ghoch 2026 — ~8,500 steps for maintenance [moderate, CONFIRMED from primary]**
Saadeddine, ... El Ghoch M, Int J Environ Res Public Health 2026;23(4):522
(PMC13116791). 14 RCTs, N=3,758 adults with obesity, mean age ~53. Group
raising steps to **~8,500/day** (8,454 loss-phase, 8,241 maintenance)
maintained loss. Verified from full text: **"for every 1000 steps per day
over baseline ... a 1.34% weight loss is achieved"** in the loss phase
(T1); the maintenance-phase (T2) coefficient is **1.10%**.
→ ~8,500 is a defensible *maintenance* anchor. KILLED en route: the "1.1-1.3%
per 1,000 steps" band and a "≥9,500-step threshold" attributed to this
paper are press artifacts (the 9,500 figure is actually Schneider 2006) —
do not use them. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13116791/

**NWCR — what maintainers actually do [moderate, selection-biased]**
National Weight Control Registry, PMC4560526. Successful maintainers
report ~2,800 kcal/wk activity (~2,500 women), ≈ walking ~28 mi/wk;
walking is the single most common activity; ~90% used diet + exercise
together, only ~9% maintained on diet alone.
→ Describes what worked for people who already succeeded; does not prove
steps cause maintenance. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4560526/

### 3. Step PATTERNS beyond the daily total

**Post-meal walking lowers the after-meal glucose rise [strong, CONFIRMED]**
Buffey et al., Sports Medicine 2022;52(8):1765-87 (systematic review).
Light-intensity walking after eating significantly attenuated
postprandial glucose vs sitting (∆ = −0.72, p<0.001) and beat standing
(∆ = −0.30, p<0.009). Bouts in included studies were short (2 min every
20 min; 5 min every 30 min). Reynolds 2016 (Diabetologia, T2D, free-living
CGM): walking 10 min after each meal cut postprandial glucose ~12% overall
and **~22% after dinner** vs one 30-min walk.
→ The best-evidenced *action* nudge we own. CAVEAT: samples are acute-lab
or T2D/older, not healthy young women. Frame softly ("smooths the after-
meal dip / less of a slump"), never as a glucose-management claim for a
non-diabetic. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9325803/ ·
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27747394/

**Breaking up prolonged sitting helps [strong direction, acute-lab]**
Dunstan 2012 (Diabetes Care): 2-min light-walk breaks every 20 min through
5 h of sitting cut glucose iAUC 24% and insulin 23% vs uninterrupted
sitting. Dempsey 2016: 3-min light-walk OR simple resistance moves every
30 min attenuated glucose/insulin/triglycerides in T2D adults. Duvivier
2021 (12 overweight women): sitting less improved insulin sensitivity ~13%,
approaching the ~20% from structured exercise.
→ "Break up long sitting" is well-supported in direction, but all acute-lab
and mostly older/dysglycemic. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3329818/

**Timing of day (morning vs evening) — [weak, HONEST GAP]**
Brooker 2023 (Obesity, RCT, N=100): modest, *similar* weight loss whether
adults exercised morning or evening. Glucose-timing effects are mixed and
mostly in T2D/metabolic-syndrome men.
→ This is the weakest topic. Do NOT claim a best time to walk. The honest
insight is reassurance: walk whenever fits your life.
https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/oby.23605

**Consistency vs volume, and the weekend gap — [weak, do not prescribe]**
dos Santos 2022 (JAMA Intern Med, N=350,978): with total volume matched,
"weekend warriors" got essentially the same mortality benefit as the
regularly active — **total volume drove the signal, not even distribution**
(corroborated device-based by Khurshid 2024, Circulation). A real weekday-
to-weekend movement dip exists descriptively (Whitaker 2022, NHANES:
adults move more on weekdays). BUT: **no robust study shows day-to-day step
*regularity* independently predicts weight or mortality once volume is
accounted for.** In the step literature, day-to-day variability is treated
as a measurement problem (need several days to estimate habitual level),
not a health exposure.
→ We may *notice* a weekend dip warmly, but we must NOT claim consistency
itself lowers risk. If anything the evidence says total volume matters more.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9257680/

### 4. NEAT — the honest framing

**NEAT is huge and variable [strong]** — Levine: non-exercise activity
thermogenesis can differ by up to ~2,000 kcal/day between similar-sized
people; in overfeeding, NEAT changes predicted resistance to fat gain
(r=0.77) and were ~2/3 of the rise in energy expenditure (Science 1999).
Lean adults stand/move ~152 min/day more than obese peers (~350 kcal/day)
(Science 2005). https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.283.5399.212

**...but the body compensates [moderate]** — Pontzer 2016 (constrained
total energy expenditure): TEE rises with activity only in the low range,
then plateaus. ~67% of studies show non-exercise movement *drops* when
people start structured exercise (compensation).
→ Honest frame: steps and incidental movement genuinely matter, but "just
add steps" gets partly offset. Position steps as "protect and stack
everyday movement," not "burn X calories." Trackers alone yield only ~1 kg
(see Ferguson below). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26832439/

### 5. Walking, GLP-1, and muscle — the careful part

**GLP-1 weight loss carries substantial lean-mass loss [strong]** — Roughly
**25-45% of weight lost on GLP-1s is lean/fat-free mass** (STEP-1
semaglutide ~40-45%; SURMOUNT-1 tirzepatide ~26%); reviewers debate
whether this is worse than any diet-induced loss.
https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.124.067676

**Resistance training is the muscle lever, not walking [strong, CONFIRMED with correction]**
Frontiers in Nutrition 2025 network meta-analysis: **resistance training
best preserves lean mass during calorie restriction.** VERIFICATION
CORRECTION: the earlier framing "aerobic ≈ no-exercise for lean mass" was
overstated — aerobic/walking added to diet still ranks *above* diet-alone
for lean-mass preservation; it is simply not as good as resistance work.
Guidance for people on GLP-1s: resistance 2-3x/week + protein ~1.2-1.6
g/kg/day. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12158682/
→ HONEST + COMPLIANCE: we must NOT imply walking preserves muscle on
GLP-1. Walking is for heart, mood, adherence, everyday energy. The muscle
message belongs to resistance + protein (which we already carry in the
protein-adequacy nets). No drug names, no drug-equivalence, no "counteracts
the medication" framing.

### 6. Step-feedback interventions — effect sizes

**Trackers work, but the weight effect is small [strong, CONFIRMED]**
Ferguson T et al., Lancet Digital Health 2022 (umbrella review, 39 reviews,
163,992 people): wearables increased activity by **~1,800 steps/day, ~40
min/day walking, and ~1 kg bodyweight**.
→ Position steps as a behavior/adherence tool, not a weight engine.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35868813/

**Feedback beats a bare goal [moderate, CONFIRMED — corrected citation]**
Strath et al., J Phys Act Health 2011: individualized motivational
*feedback* arms took **+2,159 and +2,488 steps/day** vs control, while a
pedometer + bare 10,000-step *goal* "did not differ" from control.
(Verification note: these figures belong to Strath 2011; earlier draft
mis-cited two other papers.)
→ The insight sentence, not the target number, is the active ingredient.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3292189/

**Adaptive/personal goals beat static 10k [moderate, CONFIRMED]**
Adams et al., PLoS One 2013 (N=20): adaptive percentile-rank goals +2,728
vs static-10k +1,598 = **+1,130 steps/day, Cohen's d=0.74, p=.017**. Zhou/
Fukuoka 2018 (N=64): ML personal goals netted +960 steps/day and ~2x goal
attainment vs fixed 10k. Both small-n (pilot-grade) but directionally
consistent.
→ A number drawn from *her own* last two weeks beats a number off a box.
This is a JITAI-adjacent lever we can build. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3857300/

### 7. Measurement honesty — how wrong is phone-only?

**When carried and walking normally, the phone is good [strong]** — On a
treadmill iPhone error is <±5-7% at 5-10 km/h (Duncan 2018; Case JAMA 2015).

**The phone's real error is absence, not algorithm [strong, CONFIRMED]**
Amagasa 2019 (JMIR mHealth, N=54, iPhones): iPhone undercounted free-living
steps by **~12%** (9,253 vs 10,530/day). The miss scales with carriage:
seldom-carried −3,036 vs almost-always −929 steps/day; **phone in a BAG
−2,104 vs POCKET −791 (p=.02, ~2.7x worse).** Head-to-head, phone step
apps run ~30% error vs ~6% for Apple Watch in 24-h free-living (Hong 2024);
long-term, phone apps logged ~34% fewer daily steps than a simultaneously-
worn wrist tracker (Piccinini 2020). https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6329418/

**Women 22-35 are the worst case for phone carriage [moderate]** — Redmayne
2017 (PLoS One, 197 women 15-40): 86% stored the phone off-body in the
prior week; handbag was the most common daytime location.
→ Our exact demographic most often carries the phone in a bag, exactly the
condition that undercounts most. This is a copy honesty obligation.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5218506/

**Slow walking is the second failure mode [moderate]** — ~20% of steps
missed at 2 km/h vs ~1% at 4 km/h (Presset 2018). Undercounting is the
dominant direction; overcounting from driving vibration is vendor-
acknowledged but NOT study-proven.

**Trend/threshold is the honest unit, not the exact number [strong, CONFIRMED]**
Spartano 2024 (Framingham, 523 people): even Apple Watch vs a research
accelerometer agreed on absolute daily steps only ~30% of person-days
(limits of agreement ±~9,000 steps) — yet agreed **74.8-84.5% on whether a
person MET a threshold**. Yao 2021 (212,048 adults): phone users need ~4
days/week of data (vs ~2-3 for wrist) for a stable habitual estimate.
→ Never show the day's exact count as truth. Show direction, personal
baseline, threshold-met, and multi-day trends. The noise cancels over a
week; a single day is unreliable on ANY consumer device, phones most of all.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11306942/

## LANE 2 — the product bar (what sentences these apps author)

Ranked by how *meaningful* the sentence grammar is (from the teardown):

1. **Causation prompt (Oura) — deepest.** Deviation + a gentle question:
   noting sleeping HR dropped late and asking "did you eat late?" Teaches
   the user her own patterns instead of just reporting. [product-fact]
2. **Day-matched personal baseline (Gentler Streak) — the benchmark.**
   "Below Typical Thursday." Honest about a down day, but the baseline is
   *hers* and *day-matched*, so it reads as observation, not verdict. Rest
   is folded into the streak ("Streaks that celebrate rest"). [product-fact]
3. **Long-window trend + micro-action (Apple).** Trends compares your last
   **90 days vs your last 365**, needs 180 days before it speaks, and uses
   a non-shaming taxonomy: "Keep it Going" (up) / "Worth a Look" (down,
   with one concrete tip like "walk an extra quarter of a mile a day").
   Statistically rigorous, emotionally flat. Notifications stay neutral
   ("there's been a change") and let the arrow carry direction. [product-fact]
4. **Than-usual deviation callout (Oura/Apple Highlights).** "your
   inactivity time is lower than usual." Good default grammar; "usual" must
   be her own rolling baseline. [product-fact]
5. **Evidence-based unit (Google Heart Points, Fitbit AZM).** The metric
   itself encodes the science (intensity minutes, AHA/WHO-derived) — but a
   unit with no daily narration decays into gamification. [product-fact]
6. **Threshold celebration / vanity streak (Fitbit buzz, StepsApp records)
   — NOISE.** "you did it" or nothing. The volume of community threads
   asking how to turn goal-celebration off is the empirical verdict.
   Redeemed only by forgiveness mechanics (Pedometer++ earns a Rest Day
   after 6 goal days; the streak doesn't break). [product-fact]

Two negatives to bank: **canned praise repeated verbatim** ("just the
workout you needed," fired daily) is the fastest way for a warm voice to
curdle into noise; and **neutral-register honesty** (Apple's "there's been
a change") is trustworthy but forfeits warmth. **The JeniFit opening is
Apple's baseline math delivered in Gentler Streak's register, with Oura's
occasional causation prompt** — honest statistics, warm voice, her own
baseline, a gentle question now and then, and never a shame or a vanity
buzz.

## THE HONEST STEP-INSIGHT CATALOG

Ranked for JeniFit: evidence strength × how honestly it survives phone-only
noise × meaningfulness (not vanity) × fit for women 22-35 × the "value even
when she does little" principle. Passive-value reflections lead; gentle
action nudges follow; framing/context and weak-evidence items sit last.
Every example sentence is lowercase, warm, no shame, no labor verbs, no
round-number worship, no scale promise.

**1. this week vs your usual (rolling personal-baseline trend) — [strong]**
The daily workhorse. Compare a multi-day window to her own rolling baseline,
never the raw day (survives phone noise: Spartano, Yao). Underpinned by the
whole dose-response (Lee, Paluch, Ding).
→ *"you're moving a little more this week than your usual. that's your body
getting its rhythm back."*

**2. today vs your typical [weekday] (day-matched baseline) — [strong]**
Gentler Streak's benchmark grammar; honest on a down day without a verdict.
Threshold-met framing is the measurement-honest unit (Spartano).
→ *"quieter than your usual tuesday, and that's allowed. tuesdays are like
that."*

**3. the gentle floor (benefit starts low; the round number was an ad) — [strong]**
Anti-shame, pure passive value. Banach: benefit from ~2,300-3,900 steps;
Ding: 7,000 beats 10,000 as a target; 10k is 1960s marketing.
→ *"even today's few thousand steps counts. the health curve starts way
below any number a box ever put on you."*

**4. a few minutes after a meal (post-meal walk nudge) — [strong evidence, soft frame]**
Best-evidenced action we own (Buffey, Reynolds), but samples are acute-lab/
T2D — frame as feeling, not glucose management, and never medical for a
non-diabetic.
→ *"if you've got five minutes after lunch, a slow walk helps you feel less
sluggish after. no need to change out of anything."*

**5. you've been still a while (break up long sitting) — [moderate]**
Detect a no-steps window; nudge tiny movement. Strong direction (Dunstan,
Dempsey, Duvivier), acute-lab caveat. Low effort, high dignity.
→ *"you've been sitting a good stretch. two minutes on your feet is enough
to count. stretch, refill your water, come back."*

**6. your own number, not a number off a box (adaptive goal) — [moderate]**
Personal percentile goal beats static 10k (Adams d=0.74; Zhou). JITAI-
adjacent; we can build it. Set from her last ~2 weeks.
→ *"let's aim for a number that fits your last couple weeks, not a factory
setting. today, that's a little past yesterday."*

**7. steps stack, they don't erase (NEAT honesty + adjunct framing) — [moderate]**
Levine: NEAT is huge; Pontzer/Ferguson: it compensates and trackers alone
move ~1 kg. Protects trust by not overpromising.
→ *"walking won't out-run the kitchen, and it doesn't need to. it's the
quiet stuff that stacks up in ways the scale is slow to show."*

**8. since you started (long-arc progress from her own data) — [moderate]**
Ferguson: trackers do raise steps ~1,800/day. Reflect her real change; never
attribute weight to it causally.
→ *"your everyday walking is up since we met. that's not nothing. that's a
habit forming."*

**9. your weekend rhythm (descriptive noticing only) — [weak]**
A real weekday-weekend dip exists (Whitaker), but regularity lowering risk
is UNPROVEN and volume matters more (dos Santos). Notice, never prescribe.
→ *"your weekdays and weekends move to different rhythms. no fixing needed,
just something to know about yourself."*

**10. movement while you're losing (GLP-1 / muscle context) — [moderate, compliance-bound]**
Honest redirect: walking is for heart, mood, energy; the muscle lever is
resistance + protein (which we already hold). No drug names, no equivalence.
→ *"walking's a gift to your heart and your head. to hold onto your
strength while the weight moves, that's what the strength minutes and your
protein are for."*

### the two anti-insights (things we will NOT author)
- **no "best time to walk."** Timing evidence is null/mixed (Brooker 2023).
  Reassure instead: walk whenever fits your life.
- **no "more steps = a leaner you" as fact, and no glucose claims for
  non-diabetics.** The causal adiposity evidence in women is weak-to-null
  (women-review intervention subset; ELANS null). Association only.

### the measurement-honesty disclosure (a trust feature, not fine print)
Because most of our women carry the phone in a bag (Redmayne) and the phone
undercounts most exactly then (Amagasa), we owe an honest line:
→ *"if your phone rides in your bag, we miss some steps, your real number
is a little higher than what you see here. we'd rather undercount than
flatter you."*

  women 22-35; the mortality anchors are older adults; the step-to-adiposity

  independent levers are not established.
