# Connected-patient notice — WORKING DRAFT (counsel required)

> **DRAFT.** This mirrors the in-app consent screen
> (`CareConnectionSheet` / `CareConsentView`) in plain language.
> Counsel reviews it against the executed BAA before a real pilot.
> The app is the source of truth; this document must match it.

When you connect to your clinic in the Jeni app, here is exactly what
happens.

**Who sees your information.** Only the clinic you connect to, by
entering the one-time code they give you. You always see who is asking
before anything is shared.

**What you choose to share.** Three separate choices — you can turn any
on or off:
- your **visit-prep record** (a summary of your last four weeks),
- your **daily entries** (doses you marked, how meals sat, weigh-ins),
- whether the clinic may **assign your care plan** in the app.

**How far back.** You choose: the last four weeks, or from today only.

**How long.** Until you disconnect. There is no time limit and no
automatic renewal.

**Turning it off.** One place in the app, any time, takes effect
immediately. Turning off access is separate from your treatment — your
records and any plan your clinic assigned stay with you; the clinic
simply can't see new records or make changes. For treatment questions,
contact your clinic directly.

**What this is not.** Your clinic reviews your records at your visits.
**Nothing here is watched in real time.** For anything urgent, call
your clinic or emergency services — do not use the app.

**Your records are yours.** Everything you record stays in your account
whether or not you're connected to a clinic. You can ask for a copy or
to delete your account.

**If something looks wrong.** If a medication name, dose, or schedule
your clinic entered looks wrong, you can send a correction. It goes to
your clinic to review and does not change your plan on its own. It is
not an urgent message.
