# JeniFit Science Brief (app v2)

**Purpose:** the evidence spine for coach guardrails + program copy. Every user-facing
number and claim should trace to a line here. Serves three cohorts: (1) general
weight-loss women 22–35, (2) women currently ON a GLP-1 (semaglutide/tirzepatide class),
(3) women who recently STOPPED a GLP-1 and want to keep the loss.

**Confidence legend**
- `[strong]` = RCT or meta-analysis / official guideline
- `[moderate]` = cohort, registry, DXA substudy, practice-paper / consensus
- `[weak]` = mechanistic, single small study, position stand, or narrative review
- `[verify]` = finding is directionally supported but a specific number/wording could not be
  pinned to the primary source; do not put the exact figure in user copy without re-checking

**Sourcing rule:** peer-reviewed journals, position stands (ACSM, ISSN, Academy of Nutrition
& Dietetics, Obesity Medicine Association), and NIH/CDC/FDA/FTC only. No blogs, no
supplement-industry sources. Citations below were checked against the primary journal/agency
record in July 2026. Two rigorous sources (Greendale 2019 *JCI Insight*; Levine 1999
*Science*) sit outside the named journal set and are flagged inline.

## 1. Protein on GLP-1 — preserving lean mass during pharmacotherapy

**Lean mass is a meaningful share of GLP-1 weight loss.**
- STEP 1 semaglutide DXA substudy: of ~13.6 kg lost, ~8.3 kg (62%) was fat mass and
  ~5.3 kg (38%) was lean/fat-free mass (Wilding 2021, *NEJM* 384:989–1002, DXA substudy as
  reported in the joint advisory Mozaffarian 2025, *Obesity*) `[moderate]`.
- SURMOUNT-1 tirzepatide DXA substudy (n=160, 72 wk): weight −21.3%, fat mass −33.9%, lean
  mass −10.9% — roughly **~25% of weight lost was lean tissue, ~75% fat**, a split mirrored in
  the placebo arm (Look 2025, *Diabetes Obes Metab*) `[moderate]`.
- STEP 1 and SURMOUNT-1 are **not head-to-head** (different populations, DXA methods), so treat
  the semaglutide-vs-tirzepatide lean-fraction gap cautiously; the honest coach framing is
  **"roughly 1 in 4 to 1 in 3 pounds lost can be lean tissue."** `[verify]` on any single exact %.
- *Coach use:* motivates protein + resistance training. Frame as protective, not alarming;
  never a first-party numeric weight-loss claim.

**Protein target during medication-assisted weight loss.**
- Independent 4-society joint advisory (ACLM/ASN/OMA/TOS): **1.2–1.6 g/kg/day (~80–120 g/day)**
  during active weight reduction, plus resistance training ≥3×/wk and ≥150 min/wk moderate
  aerobic activity (Mozaffarian 2025, *Obesity* 33:1475–1503, joint advisory) `[moderate]`.
- GLP-1 nutrition practice review: **≥60–75 g/day, up to ~1.5 g/kg/day**, protein eaten first at
  each meal (Almandoz 2024, *Obesity* 32:1613–1631, narrative review — **industry-funded (Eli
  Lilly), disclosed**; pair with the independent Mozaffarian advisory) `[weak]`.
- General exercising-adult range **1.4–2.0 g/kg/day**, up to **2.3–3.1 g/kg of fat-free mass** to
  retain lean mass in a deficit (Jäger 2017, ISSN Position Stand, *J Int Soc Sports Nutr*) `[weak]`.
- *Coach use:* defensible in-app target is **1.2–1.6 g/kg/day (or the simpler 80–120 g/day
  heuristic)**, spread across meals. This is the number best backed by GLP-1-specific society guidance.

**Protein alone is not enough — resistance-training synergy.**
- 40% energy deficit + 6×/wk resistance/anaerobic training: the high-protein arm (2.4 g/kg/day)
  **gained +1.2 kg lean and lost −4.8 kg fat** vs +0.1 kg lean / −3.5 kg fat at 1.2 g/kg/day
  (Longland 2016, *AJCN*) `[strong]`.
- Adding exercise to liraglutide after a low-calorie diet **preserved fat-free mass and roughly
  doubled body-fat-% reduction (−3.9 vs ~−1.8 pts)** and gave the best maintenance
  (Lundgren 2021, *NEJM*) `[strong]`.
- *Coach use:* always pair the protein target with structured resistance training; the
  combination — not protein alone — is what preserves or adds lean mass while cutting fat.

## 2. Post-GLP-1 regain — the expected pharmacology of stopping

- STEP 1 extension: after 68 wk (−17.3% on semaglutide), stopping drug + lifestyle led to regain
  of **11.6 of 17.3 percentage points (~two-thirds, ~67%) by wk 120**, with cardiometabolic gains
  reverting in parallel (Wilding 2022, *Diabetes Obes Metab* 24(8):1553–1564) `[strong]`.
- SURMOUNT-4 withdrawal RCT: after a 36-wk tirzepatide lead-in (~21% loss), switching to placebo
  **regained to +14.0% vs −5.5% continuing — a 19.4-point difference (P<0.001)**; ~82% of the
  placebo arm regained >25% of lost weight (Aronne 2024, *JAMA*) `[strong]`.
- STEP 4 withdrawal RCT: after a 20-wk semaglutide run-in (−10.6%), continuing lost a further
  −7.9% while switching to placebo **regained +6.9%** over 48 wk (~14-point swing)
  (Rubino 2021, *JAMA*) `[strong]`.
- *Coach use:* regain on stopping is **expected pharmacology, not personal failure.** Position the
  app as the durable behavioral layer that blunts the rebound; never promise off-drug maintenance.

**Published behavioral predictors of maintained loss (the levers the app can instrument).**
- NWCR successful maintainers: **high physical activity (~1 h/day), frequent self-weighing,
  consistent diet self-monitoring, low TV/sedentary time** (Wing & Phelan 2005, *AJCN*
  82(1 Suppl):222S–225S) `[moderate]`.
- Consistent self-weighing tracks with better maintenance; lapses predict regain
  (Butryn, Phelan, Hill & Wing 2007, *Obesity*) `[moderate]`.
- Continued/added exercise during and after GLP-1 therapy improves post-treatment maintenance
  (Lundgren 2021, *NEJM*) `[strong→moderate]`.
- *Coach use:* the four buildable habits — **daily movement, regular self-weighing, protein
  consistency, food logging** — are the evidence-based hedge against post-GLP-1 regain.

## 3. Weight-maintenance science (general)

- **NWCR profile:** members lost ~30 kg avg, kept it off ~5.5 yr; "successful maintenance" =
  losing ≥10% and keeping it off ≥1 yr (Wing & Hill 2001, *Annu Rev Nutr* 21:323–341) `[moderate]`.
- **Maintainer behaviors:** ~2,600 kcal/wk activity (~1 h/day walking-equivalent), consistent
  low-fat/low-calorie diet, daily breakfast (78%), self-weighing ≥1×/wk (75%), low TV time
  (Wing & Phelan 2005, *AJCN*) `[moderate]`. Exact "TV ≤10 h/wk" stat not source-pinned `[verify]`.
- **Self-weighing frequency → outcomes:** regular self-weighing is consistently associated with
  more loss / less regain and is **not** linked to anxiety, depression, or disordered eating in
  these cohorts (Zheng 2015, *Obesity* 23(2):256–265) `[moderate]`.
- **Dietary self-monitoring → outcomes:** across 15 studies, self-monitoring was significantly
  associated with weight loss; more consistent loggers lost more (Burke 2011, *J Am Diet Assoc*
  111(1):92–102) `[strong]`.
- *Coach use:* frame daily-ish weigh-ins and consistent (not perfect) eating as **maintenance**
  behaviors, not just loss-phase tactics. Scale = neutral data; watch the ED-history minority.

## 4. Safe loss pacing

- **Evidence-based pace:** a standard ~1,200 kcal/day low-calorie diet produces ~0.4–0.5 kg/wk;
  diets <800 kcal/day (VLCD) are **not** more effective and need medical supervision (cardiac /
  refeeding risk) (NHLBI/NIH Obesity Guidelines 1998; National Task Force on VLCDs 1993, *JAMA*
  270:967–974) `[strong]`. CDC echoes the **1–2 lb/wk** target `[moderate]`.
- **The "1,200 kcal" floor is a rule of convenience, not a magic threshold.** The evidence-based
  line is **<800 kcal/day = VLCD = needs supervision**; women's published safe floor is
  ~1,200–1,500 kcal/day, and <1,200 risks unmet nutrient needs (NHLBI 1998 `[strong]`;
  Almandoz 2024, *Obesity* `[moderate]`).
- *Coach use:* cap default pacing near **0.5–1%/wk**; never auto-generate sub-1,200 kcal targets
  for women without a supervision flag. Sell sustainable pace as the science-backed choice.
- **Activity dose (ACSM):** ~150–250 min/wk moderate activity prevents gain / modest loss; >250
  min/wk gives clinically significant loss; **200–300 min/wk to prevent regain** (Donnelly 2009,
  ACSM Position Stand, *Med Sci Sports Exerc* 41(2):459–471) `[strong]`.
- **Sleep shifts loss from fat to lean:** at matched ~3 kg loss over 14 days, 8.5 h sleep → 1.4 kg
  fat (56% of loss); 5.5 h sleep → only 0.6 kg fat (25%), with more lean-mass loss (Nedeltcheva
  2010, *Ann Intern Med* 153(7):435–441) `[strong, n=10]`.
- *Coach use:* sleep is a body-composition lever. Pair any aggressive-pace warning with a
  sleep prompt — under-slept hard-dieters burn muscle.
- **Perimenopause:** in SWAN, fat-mass gain roughly doubled (~1.0→1.7%/yr) and lean mass declined
  across the menopause-transition window while scale weight stayed flat — hiding the fat↑/lean↓
  trade-off (Greendale 2019, *JCI Insight* 4(5):e124865 — peer-reviewed NIH cohort, outside named
  journal set) `[moderate]`.
- *Coach use:* for perimenopausal users, prioritize protein + resistance training and de-emphasize
  the scale; the number can stay flat while composition worsens.

## 5. CBT + behavioral science

- **CBT does not durably prevent regain (the key honest caveat).** In a 150-woman RCT, new CBT vs
  behaviour therapy vs guided self-help all produced ~10% initial loss, but "the great majority
  regained almost all the weight," and CBT was **no better than behaviour therapy** at 3-yr
  follow-up (Cooper, Fairburn et al. 2010, *Behav Res Ther* 48(8):706–713) `[strong]`.
  - *Coach use:* position maintenance as the hard part; never imply a lesson course "locks in" loss.
- **Self-compassion beats shame for eating behavior.** Systematic review: self-compassion
  interventions improved eating behaviours, body image, and weight-loss attempts (Rahimi-Ardabili
  2018, *Mindfulness* 9:388–400) `[moderate]`. Pilot RCT (n=41 BED): 3-wk self-compassion training
  reduced binge days and self-criticism vs control (Kelly & Carter 2015, *Psychol Psychother*
  88(3):285–303) `[moderate]`.
  - *Coach use:* shame is a documented **risk** signal, not a motivator. Kind lapse-reframing is
    evidence-aligned copy.
- **Habit formation: median ~66 days, range 18–254.** Time to automaticity for a daily behavior in
  a fixed context (Lally 2010, *Eur J Soc Psychol* 40(6):998–1009) `[moderate]`.
  - *Coach use:* never promise "21 days"; frame as weeks-to-months with wide individual variation.
- **Implementation intentions ("if-then" plans): medium-to-large effect, d=0.65** across 94 studies
  (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, *Adv Exp Soc Psychol* 38:69–119) `[strong]`.
  - *Coach use:* prompt concrete "when X, I'll Y" plans (e.g. "when the 3pm crave hits, water first").
- **Flexible restraint > rigid restraint.** In a >54,000-person validation, **flexible** eating
  control tracked lower disinhibition, lower BMI, and more successful weight reduction; **rigid**
  all-or-nothing control tracked the opposite (Westenhoefer 1999, *Int J Eat Disord* 26(1):53–64
  — note: NOT *Appetite*) `[moderate]`.
  - *Coach use:* direct evidence **against** punitive streaks. Favor flexibility, "tomorrow resets,"
    permission framing over all-or-nothing streak-loss threats.

## 6. Food logging + photo-based tracking

- **Logging consistency → more loss (dose-response).** In a 24-wk online program, ≥10% losers
  logged ~2.7 eating episodes/day and ~20 days/month by month 6 (vs ~1.7×/day for <10% losers);
  per-log **time dropped from ~23 → ~15 min/day** as it became habitual (Harvey 2019, "Log Often,
  Lose More," *Obesity* 27(3):380–384) `[moderate]`.
  - *Coach use:* frequency beats perfection. Nudge "log each time you eat" over one nightly
    catch-up; reassure users it gets faster — a strong retention message.
- **Photo logging: evidence is thin and mixed.** Image-assisted methods **reduce user burden**
  (portion estimation shifts off the user) but "less burdensome methods are less accurate" and can
  underestimate energy/macros vs weighed records (image-assisted dietary assessment review, *Int J
  Obes* 2021; authorship `[verify]`) `[weak]`. **No strong published evidence that photo logging
  beats manual entry for adherence or weight loss.**
  - *Coach use:* sell photo capture as **lower friction**, never as "more accurate" or "clinically
    superior." Honesty protects the claim.

## 7. Breathwork + stress

- **Cyclic sighing beats mindfulness for mood/arousal.** Remote RCT (~108 completers), three
  5-min/day breathwork practices vs mindfulness over 1 month: breathwork — especially
  exhale-emphasized **cyclic sighing** — produced greater daily positive-affect gains and larger
  respiratory-rate reduction than meditation (Balban 2023, *Cell Reports Medicine* 4(1):100895)
  `[strong]`.
- **Cortisol–abdominal-fat link.** 59 premenopausal women: those with central fat secreted
  **consistently more cortisol** across repeated lab stressors (Epel 2000, *Psychosom Med*
  62(5):623–632) `[moderate]` — associational; does not show that lowering cortisol reduces fat.
- **DEFENSIBLE vs OVERREACH (read before writing breathwork copy):**
  - Defensible: breathwork **improves mood, lowers physiological arousal, reduces respiratory
    rate** (Balban 2023); chronic stress reactivity **correlates with** higher cortisol and central
    adiposity (Epel 2000). Frame as a **stress/arousal/mood** tool.
  - OVERREACH — DO NOT CLAIM: breathwork "burns fat," "lowers cortisol so you lose weight," or
    "causes weight loss." No RCT shows breathwork changes body weight or fat. Keep the existing
    "cortisol mechanism, NOT fat-burn" guardrail.

## 8. Steps — why 7,500 is a defensible anchor

- **~7,000-step inflection (middle age).** CARDIA (2,110 adults, 38–50 yr, ~10.8 yr follow-up):
  ≥7,000 vs <7,000 steps/day → 50–70% lower all-cause mortality; **no added benefit above
  ~10,000**; cadence/intensity not associated after adjusting for volume (Paluch 2021, *JAMA Netw
  Open* 4(9):e2124516) `[moderate]`.
- **Benefit plateaus below 10,000 (meta-analysis).** 15 cohorts (~47,471 adults): risk fell then
  plateaued at ~6,000–8,000 steps/day for adults ≥60 and ~8,000–10,000 for adults <60
  (Paluch 2022, *Lancet Public Health* 7(3):e219–e228) `[strong]`.
- **The "10,000" target is a 1965 Japanese pedometer marketing name** (*Manpo-kei*), not a study
  finding `[weak, marketing-origin]`.
- **NEAT:** with 1,000 kcal/day overfeeding, changes in non-exercise activity thermogenesis
  (fidgeting, posture, daily movement) accounted for ~two-thirds of the rise in energy expenditure
  and predicted fat-gain resistance (Levine 1999, *Science* 283:212–214 — peer-reviewed, outside
  named journal set) `[weak, n=16]`.
- *Coach use:* a **7,500** anchor sits inside the evidence-based sweet spot, is more attainable
  (→ better adherence), and is more defensible than 10,000. Reward total volume, not cadence.

## 9. GLP-1 side-effect-aware nutrition habits (wellness side of the line)

Context (the coach does NOT diagnose or manage these): GLP-1 receptor agonists commonly cause
mild-to-moderate, dose-dependent, usually transient GI effects — nausea, vomiting, diarrhea,
dyspepsia, constipation, reflux; in STEP 1, GI events were the most frequent adverse events, with
4.5% discontinuing vs 0.8% on placebo (Wilding 2021, *NEJM* `[strong]`; Almandoz 2024, *Obesity*
`[moderate]`). Verified general-wellness habits (frame as habits, never dosing/symptom management):

- **Hydration:** aim >2–3 L/day fluid, mostly water; GLP-1 agonism may blunt thirst, so make it a
  deliberate habit (Almandoz 2024, *Obesity*) `[moderate]`.
- **Protein first:** ≥60–75 g/day, up to ~1.5 g/kg/day; eat protein first at each meal (Almandoz
  2024) — align with the 1.2–1.6 g/kg (~80–120 g/day) advisory target (Mozaffarian 2025) `[moderate]`.
- **Gradual fiber + fluid for regularity:** ~21–25 g/day fiber (women); increase gradually with
  adequate fluid to reduce constipation; soluble gel-forming fibers (psyllium) help (Almandoz 2024;
  Mozaffarian 2025) `[moderate]`.
- **Smaller, slower, more frequent meals:** small meals every 3–4 h; stop before feeling full
  (Almandoz 2024; Mozaffarian 2025) `[moderate]`.
- **Limit nausea triggers:** high-fat/fried, spicy foods, alcohol, carbonated drinks; ginger/
  peppermint tea may help (Almandoz 2024; Mozaffarian 2025) `[moderate]`. "Limit very sweet foods"
  is common clinical advice but was NOT explicit in either primary source `[verify]`.
- **Micronutrient safety net:** deficiencies (vit D, B12, iron, folate, thiamine, zinc) are common
  in obesity and reduced intake raises risk; a complete multivitamin + calcium + vit D can be
  considered (Almandoz 2024; Mozaffarian 2025) `[moderate]`.
- **Muscle-loss rationale:** insufficient protein can drive lean-mass loss; protein alone is likely
  insufficient without resistance training ≥3×/wk + ≥150 min/wk aerobic (Mozaffarian 2025)
  `[moderate]`.
- *Coach use:* all of the above are **general wellness habits**, clearly distinct from dosing or
  medical symptom management. When symptoms are severe/persistent → route to clinician (§10).

## 10. Red lines for the app coach (compliance + safety)

**ED screening — SCOFF (what the app uses to route).** Five questions, 1 point each; **≥2 = likely
case** of anorexia or bulimia (Morgan, Reid & Lacey 1999, *BMJ* 319(7223):1467–8; original
validation sensitivity 100% / specificity 87.5%, single cohort) `[strong / moderate]`:
1. Do you make yourself **S**ick because you feel uncomfortably full?
2. Do you worry you have lost **C**ontrol over how much you eat?
3. Have you recently lost more than **O**ne stone (14 lb) in 3 months?
4. Do you believe yourself to be **F**at when others say you're too thin?
5. Would you say that **F**ood dominates your life?

Rationale for routing: anti-obesity medications are "generally inappropriate" for people with
bulimia or anorexia; BED warrants a trained mental-health professional (Almandoz 2024, *Obesity*)
`[moderate]`.

**The coach MUST NOT:**
- Make disease diagnosis / treatment / cure / mitigation claims — that removes an app from FDA's
  low-risk general-wellness enforcement discretion; weight management is acceptable only while
  lifestyle-framed (FDA 2026, "General Wellness: Policy for Low Risk Devices," final guidance,
  Jan 6 2026) `[strong]`.
- Give ANY medication instruction — dosing, titration, skipping/stacking, drug-based side-effect
  management, or stop/start advice (FDA 2026, General Wellness guidance) `[strong]`.
- Make numeric weight-loss promises or "typical results" without RCT-grade substantiation; health
  claims require "competent and reliable scientific evidence" (FTC 2022, Health Products Compliance
  Guidance — expressly covers health apps) `[strong]`. Failure mode: FTC NextMed order over
  unsubstantiated GLP-1-program loss claims + fake testimonials, $150k redress (FTC 2025) `[strong]`.
- Ever make FTC "Gut Check" always-false claims: e.g. lose ≥2 lb/wk for a month+ without diet or
  exercise; loss regardless of what you eat; permanent loss after stopping; >3 lb/wk for >4 wk
  safely (FTC 2014, "Gut Check" reference guide) `[strong]`.
- Use drug brand names, "works like/as well as," or "GLP-1 alternative"/equivalence framing on
  app-controlled surfaces (FDA 2026 warning letters to 30 telehealth companies over equivalence
  marketing, Mar 3 2026 `[strong]`; also Apple App Store 5.2.1 trademark floor `[moderate]`).
- Mishandle health data: a wellness app is a "health care provider" under the FTC Health Breach
  Notification Rule; unauthorized disclosure of identifiable health data is notifiable
  (FTC 2024, HBNR final rule, effective Jul 29 2024) `[strong]`.

**The coach MUST route to a clinician (hard handoff, stop coaching that topic) when:**
- SCOFF ≥2 or any disordered-eating signal (purging, laxative misuse, fear-driven restriction,
  bingeing) (Morgan 1999, *BMJ*; Almandoz 2024, *Obesity*) `[strong / moderate]`.
- Pregnancy, planning pregnancy, or breastfeeding — weight loss offers no benefit and may cause
  fetal harm; GLP-1 labels direct discontinuation (FDA Wegovy Prescribing Information, 2025/2026)
  `[strong]`.
- Any medication question (dose, timing, side-effect management, switching, stopping)
  (FDA 2026, General Wellness guidance) `[strong]`.
- Rapid/excessive loss markers: new amenorrhea, micronutrient deficiency, BMI <18.5, or body fat
  <15% (women) (Almandoz 2024, *Obesity*) `[moderate]`.
- Stated intake below safe floors (<1,200 kcal/day; any interest in <800 kcal/day VLCD)
  (Almandoz 2024, *Obesity*) `[moderate]`.
- Severe/persistent GI symptoms, or appetite so suppressed that eating/drinking is minimal
  (Almandoz 2024; Mozaffarian 2025) `[moderate]`.
- Medical or mental-health deterioration: dehydration signs, suspected complications, new/worsening
  depression or suicidal ideation (Almandoz 2024, *Obesity*) `[moderate]`.

## Verification caveats (do not paper over these)

- **STEP 1 lean-mass fraction (~38%)** comes from the DXA substudy as reported in the Mozaffarian
  2025 advisory; the standalone STEP 1 body-composition analysis was first released as a conference
  abstract. SURMOUNT-1 (~25% lean) is a published DXA substudy (Look 2025, DOM). The two are not
  head-to-head — use the "1 in 4 to 1 in 3 lbs" range, not a single precise %.
- **Almandoz 2024 (*Obesity*)** is peer-reviewed but Eli Lilly–funded (disclosed). Wherever
  possible, cite the independent 4-society advisory (Mozaffarian 2025) alongside or instead.
- **Westenhoefer 1999** is *Int J Eat Disord* 26:53–64, not *Appetite* (a common mis-citation).
- **CDC "1–2 lb/wk"** is standard guidance `[moderate]`; the directly verified quantitative anchor
  is NHLBI's ~0.4–0.5 kg/wk on a 1,200 kcal diet `[strong]`.
- **Photo-logging superiority** is unproven — capture is lower-friction, not more accurate. `[weak]`.
- **NWCR "TV ≤10 h/wk"** exact stat unconfirmed; direction (maintainers watch less TV) holds `[verify]`.
- **10,000-step target** is 1965 pedometer marketing, not science `[weak]`.
- Not verified / do not cite: a standalone Academy of Nutrition & Dietetics GLP-1 practice paper
  (survey stats circulate only via blogs) `[verify]`; the "limit very sweet foods" habit as a direct
  quote from a primary source `[verify]`.
- Two rigorous but out-of-set sources are flagged inline: Greendale 2019 (*JCI Insight*, SWAN
  perimenopause) and Levine 1999 (*Science*, NEAT).
